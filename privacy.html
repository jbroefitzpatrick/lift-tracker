Privacy policy

Lift Tracker · last updated November 26, 2026

Lift keeps your training, food and body data on your phone. Nothing leaves it unless you turn on an account or cloud backup. There are no ads and no tracking, and your data is never sold or shared for marketing. The one kind of analysis is described under Improving the app's estimates, and you can turn it off.

On your phone

Your plan, workouts, food log, body weight, measurements, pain reports and settings are stored in the app's storage on your phone. Progress photos are stored only on your phone and are never uploaded. Erasing all data (Settings → Backup and data) removes it from the phone.

With an account

An account is optional. With one, the app stores on Lift's server (Cloudflare):

  • the name you choose;
  • your passkeys' public keys (the private keys never leave your devices), a scrambled form of your recovery code, and scrambled forms of sign-in tokens;
  • a copy of your training, food and body data so it can sync to your other phones (not progress photos);
  • if you add friends: a weekly summary they can see (workouts done, sets, streak, strength change in percent, records without weights);
  • if you have a coach: the parts of your data you choose to share with them, plan suggestions, and notes between you;
  • posts you share on purpose (for example a new best with its weight), with any photos or videos you add, and likes and comments. A post is visible only to the friends you chose: all of them or one. Deleting a post deletes its photos and videos;
  • your profile if you make one: photo, username, bio, gym and training goal. Friends and people you exchange friend requests with can see it, and anyone who types your exact username can find it unless you turn that off;
  • your running total in a challenge you agreed to (for example minutes, miles or weight lifted), visible only to the two of you;
  • people you block, and reports you send. A report keeps a copy of what was reported so it can be reviewed, and the person reported is not told who sent it;
  • for limiting sign-ups, a scrambled form of the network address that created the account;
  • unless you turn it off, the records described under improving the app's estimates.

Synced data is protected in transit and only returned to your signed-in devices and to the people you chose to share with. It is not end-to-end encrypted, because sharing with friends and a coach needs the server to read it.

Deleting your account (Settings → Account and sync → Delete account) removes all of the above from the server right away. The data on your phone stays until you erase it.

Improving the app's estimates

To check and improve the app's estimates (your one-rep max, starting weights, how fast weights go up, how long workouts take), when you have an account the app sends the server short records with numbers only:

  • Tested maxes: the exercise, the weight you lifted, what the app had estimated just before, and the reps, reps in reserve and age in days of the set the estimate came from.
  • Starting weights: the first time you log a lift, the app's suggested weight and the weight and reps you used.
  • Progress: after the app raises a lift's weight, whether your next session reached its reps, and by how much the weight went up.
  • Workout length: the planned minutes and sets of a workout and the actual minutes and sets.
  • Reps in reserve: the reps in reserve you logged on a set next to what a recent tested max suggests.
  • Skipped or swapped exercises: which planned exercises you skipped or swapped, and for which.
  • Four-week trends: how much your squat, bench, deadlift and overhead press estimates changed, your weekly sets for each lift's main muscle, and whether a deload week fell in that time.
  • With these: the month, the exercise and its equipment type, your experience level, and, if you entered them, your sex and your age rounded down to the decade.

These records never include your name, username, notes, photos, pain reports or other health data. They are stored under a one-way code made from your account, so they can be removed when you ask, but not looked up from your name. They are analyzed only in aggregate (for example, how often a weight increase works out on average) to tune the app for everyone, and are never shown to other people or used for anything else.

Turning it off: Settings → Help improve the app's estimates. Turning it off stops sending and deletes the records you already sent. Deleting your account deletes them too. Without an account, nothing is sent.

Cloud backup

Optional and separate from accounts. Backups are encrypted on your phone with your recovery code before they are sent, so the server can't read them. Daily copies expire after 60 days.

Notifications

If you turn on notifications, your phone's push address is stored with your account so the app can send rest-timer alerts, coach notes and challenge results. Turning notifications off or deleting your account removes it.

Other services

  • GitHub Pages hosts the app itself.
  • Cloudflare runs the server for accounts, sync and backups.
  • Exercise pictures load from GitHub (the free-exercise-db project). Like any website, GitHub sees your network address when a picture loads.
  • YouTube opens only when you tap "Watch a demo".
  • Apple Health receives workout calories only if you set up the Shortcut and tap Send.

Children

Lift is not directed at children under 13, and accounts are not intended for them.

Changes and questions

If this policy changes, the date above changes with it. Questions can be raised on the project's GitHub page.
